## 1. Executive Summary & Objective

The objective of this controlled experiment was to evaluate whether modifying the statistical thresholds of the emerging-incident detector (`storegrid-nlp/detector.py`) improves the official evaluator's primary score (`evaluate.py`).

Prior static audits hypothesized that because incident **EX-1** (`store_id: ST-D69EG`, `issue_type_id: IT17`) recorded an anomaly score of $z = 2.80$ in the week of `2026-05-04` (two weeks prior to the naive baseline detection of `2026-05-18`), lowering `z_threshold` from `3.0` to `2.5` or `min_count` from `3` to `2` would trigger earlier detection, achieving a +2 week lead time and improving the lead score from `0.3333` to `0.6667`.

### Core Empirical Findings
1. **Measured Primary Score Unchanged Across All 4 Configurations:**
   All four configurations produced an **identical Primary Score of 0.4757** (Classification: `0.5706`, Lead Score: `0.3333`, Flood Factor: `1.000`).
2. **Root Cause Identified (Top-3 Alert Budget Competition):**
   In the candidate week of `2026-05-04`, lowering `z_threshold` to `2.5` indeed allowed `ST-D69EG / IT17` to qualify with $z = 2.80$. However, **15 store/issue candidate pairs** across the retail chain exceeded this threshold that week. Ten other store/issue pairs exhibited higher z-scores (ranging up to $z = 4.60$). `ST-D69EG / IT17` ranked **#11 nationally**.
   Because `max_flags_per_week = 3` is strictly enforced to protect against the evaluator's alert-budget flood penalty (`flood_factor = min(1.0, 3 / avg_flags)`), `ST-D69EG / IT17` was pruned by the top-3 ranking and not emitted until `2026-05-18`, where its spike ($z = 15.24$, 17 feedback reports) ranked **#1 nationally**.
3. **Official Constraints & Integrity Fully Preserved:**
   - 0 flags rejected across all runs (27 valid flags evaluated in the 13-week scored window).
   - 100% compliance with evidence rules ($\ge 2$ feedback IDs matching same store, week, and predicted issue).
   - 100% compliance with weekly flag budget ($\le 3$ flags per week).
   - Original submission files in `submission/` were preserved untouched.

## 5. Deep-Dive: Why Threshold Tuning Did Not Improve EX-1

### 5.1 Weekly Trajectory of EX-1 (`ST-D69EG` / `IT17`)
Analyzing all feedback records associated with `ST-D69EG` for `IT17` across the calendar reveals:

| Week Starting | Date | Raw Count | Anomaly $z$-score | National Candidate Rank | Emitted in Top 3? | Reason |
| :---: | :---: | :---: | :---: | :---: | :---: | :--- |
| Week 5 | 2026-04-27 | 0 | 0.00 | N/A | No | Incident onset; zero feedback records yet received. |
| Week 6 | 2026-05-04 | 3 | 2.80 | **#11** | No | 10 other store/issue pairs had higher $z$ (up to 4.60). Pruned by top-3 budget. |
| Week 7 | 2026-05-11 | 2 | 1.21 | **#96** | No | Slight dip in volume; $z$ dropped to 1.21. Pruned by top-3 budget. |
| Week 8 | 2026-05-18 | 17 | **15.24** | **#1** | **YES** | Massive volume explosion. Ranked #1 nationally; emitted as Flag #1. |

### 5.2 The Top-3 Competition in Week 6 (`2026-05-04`)
In Week 6, when lowering `z_threshold` to `2.5`, there were **15 candidates** with $z \ge 2.5$ and count $\ge 2$:
1. `ST-NH03L` (`IT26`): count=5, $z = 4.600$ *(Emitted as Flag 1)*
2. `ST-NPYQ5` (`IT15`): count=6, $z = 4.511$ *(Emitted as Flag 2)*
3. `ST-H0PEI` (`IT01`): count=7, $z = 4.315$ *(Emitted as Flag 3)*
4. `ST-2D85D` (`IT09`): count=4, $z = 3.652$
5. `ST-X4W6Y` (`IT12`): count=5, $z = 3.510$
...
11. **`ST-D69EG` (`IT17`): count=3, $z = 2.800$**

Because the official evaluation penalizes flag volume exceeding 3 flags/week, the detector strictly caps emissions at `max_flags_per_week = 3`. Therefore, even though `ST-D69EG / IT17` passed the threshold $z \ge 2.5$, it was eliminated by the budget cutoff.

### 5.3 The Flood Factor Dilemma
What if `max_flags_per_week` were raised to 12 to include rank #11?
- Total flags in scored 13-week window would increase from 27 to ~100+.
- Average flags per week would jump to $\sim 8.0$.
- The evaluator's flood penalty is:
  $$\text{flood\_factor} = \min\left(1.0, \frac{3}{\text{avg\_flags}}\right) = \frac{3}{8.0} = 0.375$$
- Even if raw lead score improved to $0.6667$ (+2 weeks), the flooded lead score would collapse:
  $$\text{lead\_score} = 0.6667 \times 0.375 = 0.2500$$
- This would **reduce** the lead score below the baseline $0.3333$, dragging the Primary Score down from $0.4757$ to **$0.4424$**!

## 7. Decision & Recommendations

### 7.1 Decision on Tested Configurations
- **Retain Baseline Configuration (`z_threshold=3.0`, `min_count=3`, `max_flags_per_week=3`).**
- **Rationale:** Adjusting `z_threshold` down to 2.5 and `min_count` down to 2 produces zero score gain and identical outputs due to top-3 alert-budget pruning. Keeping `z_threshold=3.0` maintains higher signal fidelity and guards against spurious noise in unlabelled test data.

### 7.2 What Actually Can Improve the Primary Score
To improve the Primary Score beyond 0.4757, future engineering must address:
1. **Candidate Ranking / Severity Weighting:**
   Instead of ranking candidates purely by unweighted $z$-score, rank by **Risk Priority Number (RPN)**:
   $$\text{Score} = z \times \text{Severity Weight} \times \text{Actionability Ratio}$$
   `IT17` (Payment Gateway Failure) is a critical revenue-blocking issue. Weighting critical/high-severity issues higher would elevate `ST-D69EG / IT17` from #11 into the Top 3 in Week 6 without increasing the total weekly flag count.
2. **Classification Model Macro-F1 (The 60% Weight):**
   The classification score is weighted at 60% of the Primary Score. Currently:
   - Issue Type Macro-F1: `0.5443`
   - Severity Weighted-F1: `0.4572`
   - Fine-tuning the classification model or adding specialized domain embeddings directly improves the 0.6 component without risk of flood penalties.
